# Language and approach panel: intentsolutions.io against the gateway vision

**Version:** 1.0 (2026-09-06)
**Type:** RA-REVW (expert panel, read-only)
**Decision context:** the Blueprint rebuild (PR #49) was reverted on the owner's call (PR #51). The existing theme stays and is tightened. This panel judged the words, the positioning, the structure of the pitch, and the calls to action of the live site (main at 4290bf4) against the vision (intent-os `000-docs/163-PP-gateway-vision/164`), the council review (`165`, sections 2, 3, 6.4), doctrine 007 (sections 5, 7.1, 7.2), and the house voice.
**Method:** one Workflow, eight seats in parallel (positioning strategist with the `positioning-angles` skill, voice expert with `brand-voice`, direct-response copywriter with `direct-response-copy`, doctrine officer, customer persona, practitioner persona, vendor partner-manager persona, approach critic), one synthesis. Nine agents, all returned.
**Companion:** `080-RA-AUDT-site-design-audit-2026-09-06.md` (the file-and-line inventory; this document is the language verdict).

## Summary


All eight seats reach the same verdict: the live intentsolutions.io is a five-line AI services brochure with a booking funnel on top, a retired-vendor logo strip under the hero, five mutually contradicting stat sets, and no sentence anywhere that says what the firm is (the gate, the standard, the evidence a stranger can check). The fix is structural, not lexical, and it is mostly subtraction: delete the slogan, the service grid, the trust bar, the hand-typed counts, the calendar links, the team grid, the dollar and timeline selects, every GCP reference, and every dash; then put on the page the parts of the vision that are true today (six rules with tests, four receipt surfaces with build-time dated placeholders, the selective admission rule, model-agnostic posture, the location) and state the parts that are still paper (one contract holder, nobody rented out, partner of record) explicitly as intent. Headline: six of eight seats ship The front door for AI implementation as H1; the two dissenters (Direct-response, Approach) want The standard is fixed. The tools are disposable. The merged recommendation ships both, the challenger in the existing eyebrow badge slot and the majority line as H1, with the gate paragraph carrying the intent qualifier the Approach critic demands. Three request CTAs replace every booking link: Request an outcome, Request access (hands off to learn.intentsolutions.io), Request partner-of-record. Page order: hero, gate in one breath, receipts, three doors, method, fixed standard, founder line, closing, footer. Two synthesizer flags for Jeremy: (1) several seats wrote founder-bio detail (military academy, Marine, restaurants, trucking) into public copy; the global standing instruction keeps that persona material out of website copy, so the merged founder line uses only what the current about page already says publicly, and the richer version needs his explicit sign-off; (2) the numbers rule as written also catches headcount and years of experience, so those are rendered as placeholders or wordless rather than typed. Every line of proposed copy below was checked for em and en dashes, dollar figures, time estimates, primacy words, cert counts, booking verbs, and the Claude plus proven, verified, or partner-of-record pairing; the marketplace tile deliberately names no vendor so the as-of date can never sit next to a vendor name.

## Consensus

- 8 of 8: the live site does not represent the vision; it is a five-line services shop with a discovery-call funnel, and the single biggest gap is that nothing on the page names the gate, the standard, or the evidence a stranger can check.
- 8 of 8: delete the H1 slogan Intelligent Solutions. Intentional Impact. and the subhead about AI-powered solutions that learn, adapt, and drive outcomes; both are interchangeable with any agency and carry no checkable claim.
- 8 of 8: remove every calendar and booking CTA on every page (two distinct calendar URLs, roughly a dozen placements) and replace the register with requests: Request an outcome (customers), Request access (members, handed to learn.intentsolutions.io), Request partner-of-record (vendors).
- 8 of 8: delete TrustBar.astro entirely; a vendor logo strip is page identity the doctrine forbids, the screen-reader text uses a banned phrase, and half the names are a torn-down estate. Vendor names survive only inside a receipt tile that links to a live artifact.
- 8 of 8: one source for every number. Hand-typed counts appear in at least five components with three different star counts and three different plugin counts; every count becomes a build-time placeholder stamped with its verified date, or it does not appear. The animated hero stat row is deleted.
- 8 of 8: every Google Cloud, Vertex AI, Firebase, BigQuery, Cloud Run, Cloud Functions, GCP, and Gemini reference goes, on every page and in every form option; grep before deploy and confirm zero hits outside git history.
- 8 of 8: the five-card What We Do grid and the three hero service columns are replaced by the gate and the doors; a service menu puts the firm in the system-integrator category the vision rejects.
- 8 of 8: keep the BuiltInTheOpen heading Proof, not promises and its four-tile, one-sentence-one-link shape; swap the Hugging Face tile for the demos catalog, drop the client name-drop, and make the lab tile say the load-bearing fact: it publishes the fail rows next to the pass rows.
- 8 of 8: the six-headshot team grid becomes a single founder paragraph (who holds the paper, and the existence proof that the door admits on receipts).
- 8 of 8: no dollar budget select, no timeline select, no project-type picker, no response-time promise anywhere; the contact form asks which door and what outcome, and prices live in the statement of work.
- 8 of 8: the about page loses Pioneering, Market Leadership, the sole-contributor claim, the cloud-partnership section, and the GCP project cards; it becomes the founder line plus the Method.
- 8 of 8: the footer loses the services column, the FAQ, the Cowork Accelerator link, and the Hugging Face link; it carries Learn, Marketplace, Labs, Catalog, Field Notes, GitHub, Contact, and Gulf Shores, Alabama.
- 8 of 8: two things named Learn say opposite things (the on-site executive onramp for non-technical buyers versus the selective learn subdomain). Nav and footer Learn point at learn.intentsolutions.io; the executive briefing, if it survives, becomes a catalog outcome under the Customers door, not a Learn.
- 8 of 8: Resellers comes out of nav and footer until a seller doctrine exists; the Cowork Accelerator third-party checkout comes off the homepage.
- 7 of 8: retire the first-person-singular Colab and Learn-with-Jeremy framing (rent-a-freelancer shape); the one reusable mechanism line (branches, reviewed pull requests, your team merges) moves into the Customers door in firm voice. The Approach seat keeps the page under a new name in firm voice rather than retiring it.
- 7 of 8: the six-rule Method, each rule with its test, goes on the homepage with the closing line that passing all six makes it Intent Solutions work whoever did it and whichever model they used.
- 6 of 8: a short Fixed standard, disposable tools section (model-agnostic by design; credentials optional, standard required; selection on evidence, never identity, tool, or pedigree).
- 8 of 8: Gulf Shores, Alabama stays in footer and contact page; a real place is a receipt.
- 8 of 8: purge every em and en dash from copy, alt text, meta descriptions, and JSON-LD before the PR gate (the doctrine seat counted forty-six outside CSS comments).
- 6 of 8: keep the honesty posture (an honest not a fit; no pressure, no pitch deck) and move it from the footer FAQ to the Customers door.
- 8 of 8: fabricated testimonials stay removed; no logo walls, no quotes; references are provided through the firm on request (vendor seat wording adopted).
- 8 of 8: the orphaned FAQSection.astro and ProjectShowcase.astro files are deleted so nobody can re-import their GCP references, stale counts, and time estimates.

### The Method

H2: Six rules. Each has a test. 1. Write the intent, the accountable party, and the failure boundary before the work. Test: could a stranger read it and say whose paper this is and who restarts if a person, vendor, or tool dies mid-engagement? 2. Enforcement travels with the code. Test: can the rule be bypassed from a different machine? If yes, it is not enforced. 3. Prove it before you call it done, and keep proving it. Test: where is the evidence, could a stranger re-run it today, is it dated, and who owns the re-check? 4. Every change explains itself to an outsider. Test: what changed, why, where it sits, how it was verified, how to roll it back, what the risks are, what is unfinished. 5. Memory is governed, cited, append-only, and replicated off the machine that wrote it. Test: if that machine died tonight, does the receipt still exist? 6. People before P&L, on the record. Test: before money moves against a named person's situation, is there a dated note that says who was asked and what the money-favorable option was? Closing line: Passes all six, it is Intent Solutions work, whoever did it and whichever model they used.

CTA: Inline text link: The method as software

Source seats: Voice, Direct-response, Doctrine, Practitioner, Vendor (near-identical); rule 4 test wording from Vendor

### Fixed standard, disposable tools

H2: What never changes, and what always does. Body: We separate what never changes (a fixed, evidenced standard) from what always changes (the model, the vendor, the tool, and the role someone plays in delivering the work), and we prove the difference on every engagement. When a model regresses or a vendor changes terms, the six rules do not move. Only the implements do. Model-agnostic by design. Credentials optional, standard required. Selection on evidence, never identity, tool, or pedigree. You are buying the standard, not a vendor.

Source seats: Positioning, Direct-response (H2), Doctrine, Vendor, Approach; closing line from Customer
